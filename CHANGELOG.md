## [Unreleased] — v0.1.0

First product cut of the Aavedak web app (2026-10-07) (local SQLite + Google Better Auth).

### Added

- **ATS — Aavedak ATS rebuilt as a best-of benchmark** — The native engine now scores 11 resume-quality criteria taken from the strongest checks across every engine: ATS parseability, sections and contact, quantified impact, action verbs (weak openers like "Assisted" or "Helped" count against you), bullet length and count, buzzwords/pronouns/passive voice, verb tense consistency, repetition and spelling, dates and gaps, length for your years of experience, and skills shown in experience. Each criterion appears in the breakdown with what it found and which tools it comes from. With a title or job description, the score is 65% match (skills, responsibilities, experience, title, and now education and certifications) and 35% resume quality.

- **ATS — years of experience** — The ATS page has a Years of experience field, filled in from your profile's experience level and editable. Aavedak ATS and the Resume Worded engine calibrate their checks to it (expected length, number of bullets, share of quantified bullets, and leadership signals from about 3 years), and Aavedak ATS compares it with the years a job description asks for. Leave it empty to score without a level. Other engines ignore it.
- **ATS — input help behind a ? icon** — The "Required by … · optional for …" lists under Target role and Job description now sit in a tooltip behind a small ? next to each label, so the form takes less space.

- **ATS — hover to see what each measure means** — Every score row and signal in the ATS detail panel (for example ATS Parse Rate, Required Skills, Job Title Match, TF-IDF similarity or Quantified bullets) now explains itself when you hover over its name, so you can tell what the number is measuring.
- **Resumes — 2 MB upload limit** — Resume PDFs can now be up to 2 MB, down from 10 MB, on the Documents page, the ATS upload and onboarding. The server enforces the same limit.

- **ATS and Jobs — preview resumes and highlight the active one** — Each resume in the ATS page's resume list and in every results table (the ATS page and a job's ATS check on Jobs) has a Preview button that opens the PDF without leaving the page. The results table is now one shared component on both pages, so they look and behave the same. The active resume's row gets a primary-coloured border around the whole row.
- **ATS — one results layout for every engine** — The resume detail view now looks the same for all 11 ATS engines: a score breakdown, strengths as pills, matched/partial/missing skills as pills, and improvements as cards with a severity tag — instead of some engines showing a plain accordion and a vague "part of the transparent weighted model" line with no detail. Engines pick up relevant context (the scoring formula, caveats) in a "Methodology & limitations" section instead.
- **ATS — Enhancv engine** — A new reference engine approximates Enhancv's free resume checker: four Content checks shown as issue counts (ATS Parse Rate, Quantifying Impact, Repetition, Spelling & Grammar), each with the specific lines flagged and a fix. With a job description, it also blends in hard-skill coverage.
- **ATS — filter engines by kind** — A Native / Reference / Open Source toggle above the engine list selects or deselects every engine of that kind at once. All 11 engines now run by default (previously only the native engine was pre-selected).
- **ATS Resume Checker — fewer false flags** — "Missing contact section" no longer fires when your email or phone number is simply in the header without a "Contact" heading, which is how most resumes are laid out. "Repeated words" no longer flags a company, college, or product name that's capitalized and legitimately repeated — only ordinary words repeated unusually often.
- **Resumes — ATS score as a pill, filename hidden on phones** — On the Documents page, each resume card now shows its ATS score as a small colored pill instead of plain text, and hides the original filename on phone screens (keeping the display name and file size) so the row stays readable.

- **Referrals — one People card with a fuse button** — The People column is now a single card. The list scrolls and the queue button stays pinned at its foot. Pressing **Queue referral** no longer opens a toast: the button turns into **Undo** with a fuse around its border that starts at the top middle and burns clockwise, all the way round, over 20 seconds, and the mail sends when it runs out. Press Undo before then and nothing is sent. Pressing the button is the confirmation, so the old "I confirm these recipients" checkbox is gone. Admins get the same button for **Scan all sources now** and **Discover people now**, with a 5-second undo; the scan confirmation dialog is replaced by it.
- **Jobs — salary and stipend on job cards** — When a company states pay for a role, the job card now shows it: on the Jobs list, in the job's details and in the Dashboard's top matches. It comes from the pay a hiring platform publishes with the posting, or a clearly labelled line in the posting itself such as "Stipend: ₹25,000/month" or "CTC: 6–8 LPA". Aavedak never estimates pay: phrases like "competitive salary" show nothing, and a pay range published for another country (for example a US or UK band on a Bengaluru role) is left out rather than shown as if it applied. Many postings for junior roles in India don't state pay, so expect the badge on only some jobs.
- **Jobs — company careers pages come first** — Each company Aavedak scans now has its own careers page tracked as the primary source of truth, alongside the hiring platform it uses. Every scan also reads the job listings the careers page publishes (one page, respecting the site's robots.txt). When the careers page lists a job, the job links there instead of to the hiring platform, and that link stays. Careers pages are found automatically when a company's job links already point to its own site (for example careers.airbnb.com), and admins can add or correct them on the Admin page. Pages that load their jobs with JavaScript publish no readable job data. For those, the hiring platform keeps supplying the jobs and the page is rechecked weekly.
- **Homepage — how it works, step by step** — The homepage now shows how a job search runs in Aavedak as one diagram, in place of the Discover, Prepare and Act cards. It starts with onboarding and your resume, then your Dashboard and finding openings, and branches into a cover letter, an ATS check you can repeat while you refine your resume, and asking for referrals. Those paths meet at Apply, then track the application and follow up. Thin curved lines link the steps, a yellow dot travels along each one to show the flow, and pairs of lines running both ways mark the steps you go back to. Each step links to its page. With reduced motion turned on, the dots stay hidden.
- **Homepage — where the roles are** — A slowly turning globe marks every city with open roles as a small gold dot, sized by how many roles are open there, next to how many cities and companies are hiring. Cities with more than one open role also get a label with their count; hover any other dot to see its city and count. Hover a city label for a quick look at its open roles; click it, or **See jobs in…**, to open Jobs searched by that city. It shows counts only, the same for every visitor, refreshes hourly and follows your light or dark theme. Drag the globe to turn it.
- **Homepage — what Aavedak promises** — A new section sets out the rules the app always follows: every job links to the company's own posting, nothing is sent until you send it from your own Gmail, new roles are matched to your skills, roles and locations with a few fresh picks each day, and nothing is guessed, neither job requirements nor anyone's email.
- **Homepage — questions** — A short FAQ at the end of the homepage answers where jobs come from, who Aavedak is for, whether it applies or sends emails for you (it doesn't), what the ATS check does and how to get access, with a link to the full How it works page. Sections on the homepage are now evenly spaced on every screen size.
- **Jobs — search and location in the link** — The search box is now kept in the page address (`?q=`), so a shared link or the homepage globe opens Jobs already searched. Searching a city name also finds its other spellings: "Bengaluru" matches postings that say "Bangalore" or "Bengaluru, KA". A new location menu next to the source filter narrows the list to one city, or to remote roles, with a count for each, also kept in the address (`?location=`). Both survive signing in from a link.
- **Theme — modern dark-first, yellow primary** — The colour theme is tuned for dark mode first: a near-black page, with cards a step lighter and the sections that hold them (lists, tracker columns) lighter again, so every surface reads as a distinct layer. Dividers are now a thin, translucent grey line instead of a flat colour. Form fields (inputs, textareas, selects, checkboxes) get their own dark fill, the darkest surface in the palette, so they read as a recessed well wherever they sit. Tabs placed over a section or a card always contrast with it, for the same reason. Text contrast is checked against WCAG AA in both themes.
- **One job card style everywhere** — Jobs and applications look the same on every page: Referrals, the Job tracker board and the Dashboard's Top new matches and Recent applications now use the Jobs page card, with the company's initials, the role, "company · location" and a row of small tags (status, dates, matched skills). On the tracker board, cards show the company, then the role, then the applied date and location, with the drag handle at the top right; on the Dashboard the match score ring sits there. The colour theme is now a warm near-black (or white) with a bright yellow accent. Cards match the page and are outlined, and sit on lighter grey sections. In the Outreach chat your messages are solid yellow bubbles and replies are grey. That applies to the Jobs and Outreach lists, the Referrals applications column, the tracker columns and the Dashboard's job lists. Contrast is checked in both themes: small grey text stays easy to read and form fields have clear outlines.
- **Jobs — add a job from its link** — Add job now opens with a link box. Paste a job's link and **Fetch details** fills in the title, company, location, link and description from the posting. It works with Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable and SmartRecruiters links, and with company career pages that publish job data. You check the details, then Create; nothing is saved until you do, and fields the posting doesn't include stay empty. LinkedIn, Naukri and Indeed don't allow automated reading, so for their links Aavedak keeps the link and you type the rest. Entering everything yourself is in an **Add manually** section below, laid out in two columns on wider screens with a full-width description.
- **Outreach — chat-style inbox** — The Outreach inbox now looks and works like a messaging app. Conversations are cards, as on the Jobs page, showing the person, company and role, a preview of the latest message, its status and whether they replied. The newest conversation sits on top. Each chat opens at the latest message over a soft patterned background that follows your light or dark theme. Your mail sits on the right with a delivery tick and replies on the left, with a date divider for each day. Messages are in time order, and a dated divider (Today, Yesterday or the date) starts each day. On phones you see one screen at a time, the list or the open chat, with a back button. The application and time filters sit behind a Filters button that opens a sheet from the bottom, so the top of the page stays short. The inbox can also open full screen on phones, like a messaging app, with the collapse button always at the top right.
- **Outreach — reply from the chat** — A message box at the bottom of each conversation lets you write your own message and attach a resume. Pressing Send turns the round button into an **Undo** with a circular fuse around it that burns clockwise from the top for 5 seconds, then delivers the message from your Gmail (Undo keeps your text in the box and sends nothing). The text is locked while the fuse burns. The message goes out as a reply in the same email thread. It appears in the chat at once as "Sending…" and gets a tick when Gmail has sent it; if it can't be queued, your text comes back to the box. The paperclip button next to Send opens a list of your resumes to choose one to attach. Chat replies are never held by the one-hour wait between follow-ups. That wait now only applies to template follow-ups from Referrals, and stops once the person has replied.
- **People — add a LinkedIn profile** — The New person and Edit person forms now have a LinkedIn field, so you can keep a contact's profile link alongside their name, company, role and email. Paste any LinkedIn profile URL (`linkedin.com/in/…`); Aavedak tidies it to a standard form, shows an "in" link next to the person, and won't let the same profile be attached to two different contacts. CSV import already accepted a `linkedin` column, and this brings the same to contacts you add by hand. Aavedak never fetches or scrapes LinkedIn — it only stores what you enter.
- **People — automatic discovery of company recruiting inboxes** — A new daily job (and an admin "Discover people now" button) finds each company's openly published recruiting inbox — addresses like `careers@`, `jobs@` or `hr@` — from the job postings Aavedak already scans, its job board, and — when those have none — the company's own website (its careers page and at most one careers or contact page linked from it, respecting robots.txt), and adds it to the shared People directory as a clearly labelled "<Company> — Careers" contact you can reach out to. Everything it stores is marked unverified. It only ever keeps an address that is actually published: Aavedak never guesses an email from someone's name, never scrapes LinkedIn or other profiles, and uses no paid lookup service — so it collects company inboxes, not the personal addresses of named individuals. An address found on a company's website is only kept if it's on that company's own domain. Most large companies don't publish such an inbox, so expect contacts mainly from smaller companies. It runs once a day automatically, and admins can trigger it on demand from the admin page, where each run now reads "152 of 165 companies checked · 3 inboxes found · 2 new contacts" instead of bare numbers (job scans read "32 of 32 boards scanned", imports "4 of 4 batches imported").
- **Jobs — contact email from the posting** — When a discovered job's description openly lists an application or recruiting address (such as `careers@` or `jobs@`), Discover now shows it as an "Apply by email" link on the job, marked "from the posting · unverified". Aavedak only ever surfaces an address that is actually printed in the posting and never guesses one from a name; when several addresses appear and none clearly marks the application inbox, none is shown.
- **People — automatic credit for replies** — When someone replies to your referral or follow-up email, Aavedak adds 1 point to their score on your behalf, separate from the up and down votes people cast. It counts once per person for each Aavedak user they reply to, and only for a real reply from their own address; out-of-office notices, auto-replies and bounces don't count. Hover over the score to see how much of it comes from replies.
- **Outreach — replies to your mails** — Optional reply detection reads replies to the referral and follow-up mails Aavedak sent from your Gmail. Replies appear under the mail with a Replied badge; press Check replies, or they are checked daily. Authorize it once from Outreach; sending keeps working without it. Aavedak only reads threads of mails it sent.

It covers the hiring-platform feeds behind company career pages, companies' own career pages that publish job data, and what isn't covered. It lists every company whose careers board is scanned, each linked to its board, and sets out which jobs are kept and how recommendations are ranked. It walks through the Referrals page step by step and covers the ATS page: its three modes, the Final score, and all 10 ATS engines (1 native, 5 reference, 4 open source) with what each measures and the inputs it needs. It also gained a "Who it's for" section and an updated workspace map that includes the Dashboard and ATS pages.

Junior openings in data and analytics, machine learning and AI, QA and testing, IT support and infrastructure, security, UI/UX and product design, and associate product manager roles are included too. ### Fixed

- **ATS — reference engines now follow their published criteria** — Resume Worded scores the share of checks passed, as Resume Worded describes, and now also checks weak verb openers, tense, repetition, pronouns, spelling and teamwork signals. Teal's Resume Score is now the share of Teal's documented Resume Analyzer issue cards cleared, and its Job Match is the share of the job's keywords on your resume. Enhancv adds its bullet-length check and uses soft skills, action verbs and title when tailoring to a job. SkillSyncer's point split is now labelled as an estimate. Indian degrees (B.Tech, B.E., BCA, M.Tech, MCA, …) are now recognised for degree matching.
- **Resumes — card ATS score now matches the ATS page** — The ATS pill on a resume card came from a separate quick checklist (text length, email, phone, section names, word count, links) that almost any real resume maxed out at 100. It now uses the native Aavedak ATS engine with the same inputs the ATS page starts with (your first preferred role, your years of experience, no job description), so both show the same score. Existing cards are rescored automatically the next time you open Documents, and again whenever your preferred role or experience level changes.

- **Undo buttons keep their shape** — While a send or queue button counts down, it stays exactly the size it was, and its burning fuse now runs along the button's own border with the same rounded corners, instead of a second, square line just inside it. The fuse is a little thicker so it's easy to see, and takes the button's colour: the brand yellow, or red on a destructive button.
- **Outreach — bounces aren't replies** — A "delivery failed" bounce (from `mailer-daemon@googlemail.com` and similar), an auto-reply or an out-of-office notice no longer marks a conversation as Replied, isn't counted in its replies, and never adds to the person's score. A bounce usually means the email address was wrong, so it says nothing good about the person. You still see it in the chat, labelled **Automated · not a reply**, and the conversation card's preview starts with "Automated:". Bounces already stored are corrected, and any points they gave are removed.
- **Gmail notices** — The Gmail authorization notice is the Vinyaas alert with its default styling, like the other notices.
- **Buttons while loading** — Buttons keep the width of the wider of their label and loading text (for example Save and Saving…), so Create, Add and Save no longer shift in the Add job, Add person and Paste JD dialogs. Those footers use the Vinyaas dialog footer.
- **Layout and toggle fixes** — Jobs and Inbox panes keep a fixed height instead of stretching to the page, the Jobs loading state matches the new split layout, application cards on the job tracker show the company with a role pill and the location with the applied date, the cover letter editor has the same variables info icon as the referral email editor, the ⌘K palette footer shows just ⌘K, and single-choice controls (chart range, inbox duration, Jobs sort) are single-select toggle groups.
- **ATS — long engine names keep the table centred** — Engine names that run long, such as Hybrid Resume Analyzer, wrap onto two lines in their column header instead of pushing the header off centre.
- **ATS and Jobs — final score reads as one unit** — Each Final score shows the ring and then its verdict, centred in the column. The rings line up in one vertical line whatever the verdict's length.
- **Jobs full-screen board** — Both panels now fill the full height, and "About the job" stretches to use the spare space.
- **Referrals loading state** — The loading placeholder now matches the real column widths, with the middle column twice as wide.
- **Outreach inbox layout** — The inbox now matches Jobs: a top strip with search, application filter, time range, Process due queue and expand, then the conversation list and the mail as two separate panels. The time-range buttons no longer stack vertically.
- **Frozen resume column** — On the ATS page and in the Jobs resume check, the resume column stays put while engine columns scroll, and its divider no longer scrolls away.
- **Admin — starting a job scan** — Starting the first scan no longer shows a false "service unavailable" error, and clicking it again resumes the scan in progress instead of starting a duplicate. First-time setup of the job sources is much faster.
- **ATS page loading state** — The loading skeleton now matches the ATS page: the five step cards (engines, resume, job content, review, results) and the scoring guide, instead of a generic placeholder.
- **Local development: pages losing all styles** — Running a production build while the dev server was up overwrote the files the dev server serves, so pages lost their styles until a restart. Development and production builds now keep their files apart.

### Removed

- **Sample and demo jobs** — The Jobs page no longer creates example jobs, and existing ones are deleted automatically. Applications or cover letters you made from them stay, without the job link.
